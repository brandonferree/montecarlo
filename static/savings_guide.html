Savings Goal Calculator

A plain-language guide to the inputs and what the results mean.

This tool answers a single question in reverse: how much do you need to save each year to reach a target retirement income with a chosen level of confidence? You tell it the income you want; it works backward through thousands of simulated market futures to find the required annual savings.

How it works. The calculator tries different savings amounts and, for each, simulates your full lifecycle — saving years, then retirement spending years. It keeps searching until it finds the smallest yearly savings that still hits your target chance of success.

The inputs, one by one

Plan basics (sidebar)

Current Savings
What you've already set aside for retirement today.
Inflation Rate
How fast prices rise each year. Your desired income is entered in today's dollars and grown by this rate, so it keeps its real purchasing power. 3% is a common estimate.
Enter income amounts as (Annual / Monthly)
Convenience setting for typing your desired income — "Monthly" multiplies by 12 behind the scenes.
Target Success Probability confidence dial
How sure you want to be that the money lasts. 80% means you want the plan to succeed in at least 80 out of 100 simulated futures. A higher target is more cautious — and requires saving more.
Return Assumptions
How stocks and bonds are expected to perform. The BCM 2026 CMAs preset uses Becker Capital's own 10-year estimates; "Custom" lets your advisor adjust them.

Goal-Planning Scenarios

Compare up to three — for example, retiring in 10, 20, or 30 years, or different income targets.

Years to Retirement
How many years you'll keep saving before retirement begins.
Years in Retirement
How many years of income the plan must cover after you stop working.
Desired Income
The annual (or monthly) income you want in retirement, in today's dollars.
Accumulation Equity %
The share in stocks while you're saving; the rest is bonds. Higher equity means more growth potential and more volatility.
Glide path & Retirement Equity %
Optional. When on, the portfolio shifts to a more conservative stock/bond mix once retirement starts — a common way to reduce risk as you begin drawing income.

Note / Other Income (optional)

Income streams
One-time or multi-year inflows shared across scenarios — a property sale, inheritance, or note. These reduce the savings you need. Enter the annual amount (today's dollars), start year, and number of years (1 = a single lump sum).

How to read the results

Required Savings (headline)
The minimum amount to save each year to hit your target confidence, for each scenario.
Required Savings by Confidence (70 / 80 / 90%)
The same answer at three confidence levels, so you can see the cost of being more or less cautious.
Chance of Success & Median at Retirement
The achieved probability of the plan lasting, and the typical portfolio value at the start of retirement.
Lifecycle chart
The portfolio's path through both saving and spending phases, with the dotted line marking where retirement begins.
"Capped" note
If you see this, the target may not be reachable with the given inputs — consider a longer time horizon, a lower income target, or a different allocation.

Important

This tool is for education and illustration only. Results depend on the assumptions you enter and are not a guarantee of future outcomes or a recommendation of any strategy. Review your plan with your Becker Capital advisor before acting.
